## Numbered findings

1. **Important — R179-1: handler termination is incorrectly accepted as positive TLS peer-closure evidence.** `app/src/androidTest/java/uk/co/traynor/privategallery/core/evidence/EvidenceNetwork179Test.kt:63–66`, consumed by the new assertion at line142. The held-response path now calls `check(input.read()<0)`, but both a failed check and a socket read timeout are caught by `catch(_:Exception){}`. The unconditional `peerClosed.countDown()` in `finally` then signals precisely the same outcome as a genuine peer EOF. A server-side error/local close can therefore satisfy the new assertion without observing cancellation close the client socket. The existing CancellationException/no-publication assertions do not identify which side closed the connection.

   Concrete reproduction of the evidence defect: reach `HOLD_RESPONSE`, leave the peer connected and send no further bytes until the server's 10-second `soTimeout` expires; the handler swallows `SocketTimeoutException`, closes its own socket, and lowers `peerClosed` despite no peer EOF. Alternatively an extra inbound byte makes `check` fail and produces the same signal immediately. The full test's timing/structured-cancellation checks often reject those paths independently, but they do not make this latch a positive close observation; scheduler timing must not be the only distinction. This finding concerns the added claimed proof, not proof that the production cancellation currently leaks a socket.

   Required correction: separate handler-completed cleanup from peer-close observation. Signal the latter only after observed EOF or an explicitly classified peer-reset outcome; retain timeout/unexpected-data/other-handler failures and make the response-read test reject them. Keep the real client `responseBodyStart` precondition, cancellation classification, one-request and no-publication assertions. Do not release a server-side latch to manufacture closure. A narrow negative probe should show an open idle peer or unexpected inbound data cannot produce the positive observation.
