## Architectural review — post-kill cached restart

Read current df9b7b8 broker/runtime retirement and admission source in response to reported Android observations: the claimed PID dies synchronously, Android starts a replacement top-Activity process, cancels the Activity before onCreate, and leaves a cached child process. No reaper implementation existed in the inspected working delta; this note assesses the proposed architecture, not its correctness after implementation.

A bounded owner-held retirement reaper is appropriate as a supplement to the unchanged synchronous first kill. Activity.finish cannot clean a replacement process that receives no Activity lifecycle. The reaper must freshly enumerate only this application UID and exact dedicated browser process name, exclude the parent PID, and never kill a stale cached PID after identity may have changed. Inventory failure/null is unknown, not proof of absence. Task removal may use only an exact child baseActivity match; a topActivity-only match may belong to Main's shared task and must not authorize removing that task.

The key Important implementation risk is admission racing a stale retirement sweep. Current death handling releases processOwner promptly. If that behavior survives unchanged while delayed old-owner sweeps are introduced, a new authorized browser could start under the same process name and be killed by the old sweep. Retirement ownership/epoch must close new admission before replacement launch and remain held through stable process/task absence; every queued sweep must verify that exact ownership. Do not acquire broker/session locks or enumerate processes/tasks inside the synchronous HiddenSession revocation callback. Preserve its immediate atomic invalidation/claimed-process kill and enqueue preallocated cleanup. At the bounded deadline, unresolved/unknown retirement must remain fail-closed rather than release admission or claim success. Tor service/controller retirement requires its own lifetime proof and must not be swept indiscriminately by the browser broker.

These requirements were sent to the native implementer and coordinator. Source and actual Android verification of the eventual reaper remain outstanding; no acceptance or signing approval follows from this design assessment.
